who runs this thing anyway?

As you might have guessed, my name is ROB! I'm an alien-puppy-zombie-robot-thing trapped in the body of a sweaty, pasty earthling nerd. I crashlanded here 2006 in the Pacific Northwest and am currently attending what you humans call "college", but when I'm not doing that, you can find me floating languorously in my green goo tube, tumbling down useless research rabbit holes, or working on my various projects.

I've been drawing since I could hold a pencil. I consider illustration and writing to be my main mediums, but I also dabble in cartooning, mixed media, animation, 3D modeling, character design, fursuit making, DIY, and of course HTML/CSS :-} I'm trying to get into making music as well and would love to put a song or two out there eventually (but probably not anytime soon until I get an actual grip on what I'm doing).

I'm also a furry and therian, if you couldn't tell. I've been involved in the communities for 10+ years and it's shaped much of my artistic and personal development. You can click here to read about my fursonas!

I struggle with talking about myself in any extended capacity, so consider this entire site an exercise in humility. I'm learning to be myself without inhibition and I hope you can too. #Letsunlearnshametogether

about the site

Why "ROBGARAGE"?
Short answer: I'm Rob, and this is my garage.
Long answer: Much of my childhood was spent making stuff in my dad's garage, which has led me to associate garages as a concept with relaxation and creativity. A garage is a liminal space where time comes to a standstill and the pressure that comes with being human is lifted, temporarily, for as long as you exist in the space. It's an ethereal state of mind as much as a physical room. At risk of sounding corny, this is exactly what my site means to me and why you can find me in a dank room hunched over a bag of almonds scribbling manically in my sketchbook when you peek in my source code.

Why ROBGARAGE?
I think I speak for a lot of people when I say that I made a website because I needed an escape from social media's shittiness. Beyond its surface-level functions (portfolio, archive, fun hobby), this place is a virtual world for me to explore and express myself through, as well as a respite from being exposed to constant streams of information. Life moves at the speed of light everywhere else online, but here I can slow down and appreciate existence in my own serene corner. In a place as chaotic as the internet, aren't we all searching for that sense of serenity?

history

ROBGARAGE began when I took up HTML as a hobby in my sophomore year. The pandemic still had the world gripped tight in its iron clutches, so I had lots of time on my hands to dedicate to learning something new. And so began my webcrafting journey!

Here's a glimpse of how the site looked at first. Each page followed the same top-down layout, since I hadn't figured out how to do anything else yet and the majority of CSS positioning remained a mystery to me.
Earliest archive, 2/01/2022

I'd never heard of the small/personal/indie web before, but I couldn't have discovered it at a better time in my life. Too young to have experienced Geocities or Angelfire in their respective heydays, but juuuust old enough to witness mainstream internet mutate into the corporate hellscape we know today, it was a strange time to be a teen online. I was sick of follower counts and shallow interactions and cookie-cutter profile templates that allowed only the bare minimum for personalization. There had to be more to the internet than that, right?

I'd whipped up sites on Carrd, Strawpage, and other drag-and-drop site makers before, but none of them scratched that itch. There was (and still is) a widespread culture of using these essentially as glorified linkdumps, and it didn't help that many of them lock basic functionalities behind a paywall. Needless to say, those restrictions left me longing for more independence still. Then I discovered Neocities from an artist I followed, and through it, the small web!

It started as a pet project, but snowballed into something more special as I realized I was actually having a lot of fun. Every obstacle felt like a challenge to overcome, and the wave of euphoria that hit when i ironed out a kink or learned a quicker way of doing something made every moment of frustration worth it and more in the end. I tend to pick up and drop hobbies sporadically, but this was a rare case where one ended up sticking.

It entered a period of inactivity lasting from 2022 to early 2026, during which a lot of big events in my life happened in dizzying succession. I fell in love with my boyfriend, started HRT, became a legal adult, graduated, volunteered, moved apartments, struggled with self harm and mental illness, entered college, got my license, recovered from surgery... you get the idea. All of it burnt me out on updating for a while.

As you'd know if you've ever suffered art block, the only way out is through. I spent that 4-year liminal in a reflective state, venting my frustration through other creative outlets and slowly rebuilding my energy. It took a while until i could come back here with the passion I once had, but it was a necessary sabbatical that gave me a fresh perspective on webcraft when I returned.

All this is to say that what's on your computer screen right now is a long labor of love. I hope your time here can inspire or help you in any way.

future??????

ROBGARAGE will forever be a work in progress, as it's a direct extension of myself, so keep in mind what you're looking at is more like a sculpture of sand than one of marble. I don't expect to "finish" it any time soon and I most likely never will.

Most things on the web are transient, but I still hope one day in the distant future ROBGARAGE can be discovered on a beat-up hard drive and studied by archaeologists or something. (To the possible future archaeologist reading this: hi!)
